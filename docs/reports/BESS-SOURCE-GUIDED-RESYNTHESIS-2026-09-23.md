# Source-guided engine resynthesis — 2026-09-23

## Listening evidence and diagnosis

After trying the installed R6 fleet, the user reported that the added intake still sounds artificial and asked BESS to use the Automation export as a starting reference for a more independent engine synthesis. This is a listening verdict, not a defect inferred from signal statistics.

The Automation vehicle archives provide an exhaust sound bank, not an isolated engine-bay or intake recording. R6's engine-side stem was mostly a high-passed exhaust residual with a one-720-degree-cycle subtraction. At 5,338 rpm the subtraction makes a comb with teeth about 44.5 Hz apart, plausibly contributing to its hollow texture. The actual intake runner signal entered that stem at only one tenth of its modeled level. At the same time, the calibrated exhaust path retained a large dry copy of the source. A 16-second Cerberus A/B render measured 0.884 waveform correlation after level matching; that shows how much of the source survived, although correlation alone does not measure realism.

The engine stem was then normalized toward an exhaust-relative target at every RPM/load knot. Across the twelve R6 variants, 621 of 688 engine knots reached the 12× gain cap. This often amplified weak inferred detail rather than recovering a measured intake. The normal BESS listening mix also differed from the two stems exported to BeamNG, so local audition could mislead the user about the added engine layer.

The Cerberus off-load pair around 4,989–5,338 rpm also showed a phase-alignment weakness. Matching every prepared recording to the first low-RPM sample left these neighboring recordings poorly aligned. A diagnostic midpoint test at 5,200 rpm measured a roughly 3.6 dB drop for this pair in a simplified linear blend. Optimizing each RPM pair alone improved the 64 same-load Cerberus pairs but worsened 29 of 33 cross-load transitions; the worst cross-load profile dip moved from -3.609 to -5.156 dB. The unsafe local alignment change was discarded. A later correction needs joint RPM/load phase optimization, full-WAV checks and in-game validation.

Period analysis rejected 27 of 688 RPM/load samples across ten of the twelve source banks. At a rejected point, the existing decomposition returns the entire exhaust recording as the "residual" because no periodic template was accepted. Nineteen of those 27 rejected samples still have strong one-cycle correlation. A reliability value now follows the same RPM/load interpolation as playback, so the inferred engine layer can be reduced at these points instead of promoting a filtered exhaust copy.

An import-time median template using each WAV's measured period was prototyped as a separate inferred texture buffer. On Cerberus at 5,338 rpm it attenuated the sixth order to 17.2% of its source amplitude off-load and 12.3% on-load, but the on-load texture still correlated 0.793 with the exhaust recording. In the rendered engine stem, it increased engine/exhaust waveform correlation to 0.651 and left 37.4% of narrowband order energy, compared with 0.426 and 0.76% for the faster adaptive phase-template candidate under the same diagnostic. That diagnostic sums the first 36 engine orders in narrow ±0.65 Hz bands and is not numerically interchangeable with the broader firing-harmonic share reported for R6. Two rejected on-load knots around 1,126 and 1,205 rpm became silent between 0.55-ratio neighbors, creating a likely audible intake gap. The direct per-knot texture path was therefore rejected; the unchanged source and exhaust playback remain the reference. Optimized Cerberus import added about 0.19 seconds for this prototype, so quality rather than processing time was the deciding limit.

## Acoustic basis and limit

[BeamNG's engine-audio guide](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/sections/sounds/engine_audio/) distinguishes engine-side mechanical/induction and exhaust emitters and recommends slow RPM sweeps to detect hollow or phase-affected blends. [Davies and Harrison's intake-noise synthesis work](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/POAL%20DAVIES%20MF%20HARRISON%20HYBRID%20SYSTEMS%20FOR%20I.C.%20ENGINE%20BREATHING%20NOISE%20SYNTHESIS.pdf) models valve-flow excitation and breathing-system acoustics separately. [Spectral modeling of vehicle pass-bys](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/6/1/5) and [measured-spectrum engine synthesis](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/5209207) separate ordered content from stochastic noise shaped by the recorded spectrum. These support a source-guided decomposition and separate acoustic paths. They do not make an actual intake response or valve event history identifiable from an exhaust-only WAV.

The R7 prototype therefore treats Automation's WAVs as references for cycle timing, spectral grain and level, then uses independently controllable pressure, acoustic and mechanical paths. An authentic intake timbre would still require a separate engine-bay/intake recording or better physical measurements. Signal tests and ZIP checks cannot establish the user's preference in BeamNG.

## Chosen local candidate and verification

R7 removes R6's one-cycle subtraction, feeds the intake duct from an adaptive cycle-template residual, and reads its source texture seven engine cycles away from the exhaust stem. The adaptive template learns about half of a cycle's observations per phase bin; it subtracts persistent crank-locked content without boosting alternating cycles as the old fixed delay did. The imported WAV still determines timing, pressure shape and texture, but the calibrated B path now gives the modeled acoustic path roughly 94% of its output instead of passing through almost all of the dry source. A stays byte-identical in the 16-second Cerberus comparison.

At Cerberus 5,338 rpm/full load, the seven-cycle shift reduced engine/exhaust waveform correlation from the unshifted candidate's +0.426 to -0.205 (absolute 0.205). The narrowband order share stayed effectively unchanged at 0.76–0.79%, and engine RMS did not fall. The same diagnostic at off-load moved correlation from +0.096 to +0.020. These figures compare candidate exports under one analysis method; they are not a hearing score or a BeamNG mix measurement. A simple nominal-gain sum did not show more 50 ms volume modulation than R6 at 5,338 rpm, though the isolated under-hood stem can sound different.

The 66 Cerberus engine WAVs have no silent points and peak at 0.166 before BeamNG's JBeam gains. Three loop boundaries exceed their own within-loop 95th-percentile sample difference; the worst is 1.80 times that difference at 2,536 rpm on-load, where the absolute step is 0.0159. Maximum first/second-half RMS drift is 0.402 dB. The existing held 5,200 rpm full/low-load regression passes. A ten-second silent-output device run opened 48 kHz and completed 1,000 callbacks, with 2.002 ms maximum callback computation and no counted overruns. This tests computation and the local audio device, not in-game sound.

Two on-load source points around 1,126 and 1,205 rpm have rejected period templates. R7's reliability gate makes their inferred engine layer about 16.48 dB lower relative to adjacent accepted points. This is safer than amplifying the full exhaust copy as intake, but it may cause an under-hood intake dip in a slow low-RPM sweep. Keep it open for listening and a later interpolation or better source-recording solution.

The gate was checked across all 27 rejected knots and 36 nearest accepted neighbors in the twelve source vehicles; see `output/fleet_rejected_audit_agent/metrics.json`. The rejected median narrowband order share is 1.73%, but Archo and Thunderhawk each reach about 15.8% at 1,289 rpm off-load, compared with 2.5–3.1% in their nearest accepted neighbors. Advent and Berlingo also have rejected engine/exhaust correlation magnitudes around 0.26. Raising the gate floor from 0.15 to 0.35 everywhere would increase those unwanted hum components by about 7.4 dB. The conservative global floor remains 0.15; a later per-knot gate should use measured leakage rather than a vehicle-specific exception.

## Export level calibration and listening preview

The first R7 selectable Cerberus export was several decibels quieter in exhaust RMS than Automation at many knots, which would confound an in-game timbre comparison. The selectable two-emitter exporter now measures the original and rendered exhaust at each RPM/load knot, targets 1 dB below the original's AC RMS, limits per-knot gain to 0.5–2.5, and gives absolute sample peaks a 0.94 ceiling. A final archive-wide 0.95 ceiling remains. The older single-emitter full replacement path does not use this new calibration. The engine stem is balanced against the actual calibrated PCM24 exhaust at each knot. The level analyzer runs the same gain decisions and has an archive-level regression check.

Source WAV DC offset must be removed from RMS. A scan of 688 Automation source knots found 121 where full RMS exceeded AC RMS by more than 0.2 dB and 42 by more than 1 dB; the largest difference was 9.40 dB on a Genesis off-load point. Calibrating to full RMS would boost BESS based on inaudible energy. The UI therefore labels AC RMS explicitly, while peak headroom is still measured from absolute PCM samples. The live two-emitter preview follows the same gain rule with smoothed online source/exhaust energy and a roughly two-second rolling exhaust peak; it cannot reproduce every offline knot or BeamNG's spatial, cabin and mixer effects. On this twelve-vehicle source corpus, the original exhaust JBeam mainGain is +6 dB and the BESS engine part uses -2 dB, so the preview's nominal relative engine gain is -8 dB.

The final local Cerberus candidate at `output/cerberus-r7-ac-calibrated-20260923` has 66 exhaust and 66 engine mono 48 kHz / PCM24 loops, valid ZIP CRC, no paths shared with the original mod and an unchanged installed original SHA-256. All 66 exhaust AC RMS values are about -1.000 dB against their corresponding Automation WAVs. Maximum exhaust/engine absolute peaks are 0.2883/0.2217. A simplified four-second, pitch-matched linear blend at 5,200 rpm has an exhaust RMS dip of -1.428 dB off-load and -2.495 dB on-load; relative to installed R6 this is 1.296 dB better off-load and 0.145 dB worse on-load. The source itself measured -2.646/-2.783 dB under that model. This calculation does not include BeamNG's interpolation law or spatial mix. An additional Genesis candidate, whose sources have strong DC offset, spans -1.00022 to -1.00001 dB across 68 exhaust knots with a 0.136 peak and valid ZIP CRC. A Berlingo candidate, with DC offset as high as 0.138 in an original WAV, spans -1.00014 to -1.00000 dB across 66 exhaust knots; its highest sample is 0.801, below the 0.94 ceiling. Both ZIPs have valid CRC and no original-path overlap.
