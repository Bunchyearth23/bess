# Procedural engine experiment R8 — 2026-09-23

## Why this experiment exists

After hearing the locally installed R7 Cerberus variant in BeamNG, the user still described it as metallic and too similar to the Automation sound. This is direct listening feedback. A signal audit of that variant helps explain the complaint but does not replace it: R7's exhaust excitation still contains the imported periodic waveform and its complementary residual. The Cerberus configuration retains Automation's +6 dB exhaust main gain and uses -2 dB for the added engine emitter. Near 5,200 rpm, the engine WAV is nominally about 24–25 dB below exhaust off-load and 13 dB below on-load after that gain difference, before BeamNG's spatial processing. At 5,338 rpm/full load, the R7 exhaust moved more energy into the 500–2,000 Hz band than the source WAV (72.4% versus 53.5% of the measured 80–10,000 Hz power). These facts make familiar exhaust character and mid-band coloration plausible, but do not identify the perceptual cause on their own.

R8 is an **experimental alternative** to the source-guided R7 path. It aims to learn bounded descriptors from the Automation ZIP and generate new audio excitation, so the imported PCM is a reference rather than the audible B waveform. The existing A source reference and R7 mode remain available for comparison. R8 has not passed the user's BeamNG listening gate.

## Current model and its limits

The importer measures each original RPM/load knot: AC level, a cycle-aligned periodic component, the first four firing-order magnitudes, residual energy, a coarse five-band spectral profile, and a bounded pulse width. The generated voice makes an even-spaced event train from the available cylinder count, shapes its pressure pulse and lower order magnitudes toward the measured periodic component, and excites separate exhaust flow, intake and mechanical proxies. Noise and event strengths vary independently, while load gates the flow and intake. The model does not copy source samples into its generated excitation or follow the raw WAV envelope for pure B level matching. A/B comparison still reads the original bank for A and for a short transition when switching modes.

The Automation ZIP provides an exhaust bank and, when its `.car` metadata can be verified against the active vehicle, cylinder count and layout. It does **not** provide an isolated intake or engine-bay recording, a manufacturer firing order, cylinder pressure, runner geometry or microphone response. R8 therefore estimates the induction and mechanical character. Even-spaced events are an explicit modeling assumption, not a recovered firing order. A separate engine-bay/intake recording or more engine-specific measurements would be needed to reproduce those details faithfully.

The selectable BeamNG add-on still exports separate exhaust and engine WAV banks and uses the game's engine and exhaust emitters. Delivered WAVs remain mono 48 kHz / PCM24. The export writes steady RPM/load loops; BESS's standalone driving transients do not become a BeamNG runtime controller. A different generated waveform can still sound repetitive when its two- or four-second loop repeats in game.

For R8, the BeamNG level panel's primary exhaust comparison follows the export's nominal 80 Hz high-pass proxy: it filters the original WAV and the final quantized PCM24 after bank-wide safety gain. The detailed AC RMS and peak rows remain measurements of the unfiltered files. A focused exporter/report parity test passed on a synthetic ZIP; the proxy does not reproduce BeamNG's exact filter slope, cabin effects or spatial mix. R7 retains its earlier unfiltered AC RMS comparison.

## Final technical measurements (not sound acceptance)

The final source, R7 and R8 six-second held renders were level matched by settled full-band AC RMS. At Cerberus 5,200 rpm/full load, 80–500 Hz occupies 51.7% of the source's measured 80–10,000 Hz power, 27.3% in R7 and 27.0% in R8; 2–5 kHz occupies 2.8%, 4.7% and 5.5% respectively. The R8 full-load tone remains too mid-forward by this measure. At light load, R8 has 95.8% in 80–500 Hz versus 97.5% in the source. The 50 ms RMS p95–p5 range is 0.77 dB for R8 versus 2.02 dB for the full-load source, which may still sound too regular. See `output/cerberus-r8-final-5200-{full,light}-metrics-20260923.json`.

In the final 16-second Cerberus dynamic render, the maximum absolute waveform correlation to the source within ±50 ms is 0.401 for R8 versus 0.699 for R7, at matched AC RMS (`output/cerberus-r8-final-dynamic-metrics-20260923.json`). This verifies a changed waveform. It does **not** measure realism or prove perceptual improvement. The A source WAV remains identical to the earlier R7 comparison.

At Berlingo 1,800 rpm/0.7 load, the final R8 80–500 Hz share is 41.4% versus 39.3% in the source; 2–5 kHz is 16.9% versus 26.5% (`output/berlingo-r8-final-1800-load07-metrics-20260923.json`). This is much closer than the first warm-tone prototype but still lacks some irregular upper texture. The held comparison's full-band AC RMS match differs from the selectable export's 80 Hz-aware gain policy; neither is a BeamNG recording.

The final selectable Cerberus candidate is `output/cerberus-r8-review-20260923/bess-variant-cerberus_a-06ab054910.zip`. An independent archive check found 138 entries, 132 nonempty mono 48 kHz/PCM24 WAVs, valid CRCs and no paths shared with the original vehicle. The installed Automation ZIP still matches the local source hash, and the locally installed R7 add-on remains unchanged. The candidate was **not installed** in BeamNG. The final code passed 77 release tests and strict Clippy. A ten-second muted real-device check used the default 48 kHz stereo float endpoint: 1,000 callbacks, 0.790 ms maximum callback CPU time, zero budget overruns (`output/r8-final-audio-check.txt`). This is a short transport check, not an extended listening or performance session.

### Cross-vehicle diagnostic: Berlingo

The first warm-tone Berlingo trial at 1,800 rpm and 0.7 load exposed a different failure from Cerberus (`output/r8-warmshelf-berlingo-1800-metrics-20260923.json`). At matched full-band AC RMS, 89.4% of R8's measured 80–10,000 Hz power sat in 80–500 Hz versus 39.3% for its Automation reference; R8 had 0.67% in 1–4 kHz versus the source's 24.9%. An FFT band check of those six-second WAVs found R8 80–500 Hz RMS about 12.6 dB above the source and 2–5 kHz about 13.0 dB below it. This is a diagnostic of that **earlier trial**, not a final R8 result.

The active engine JBeam of each of the twelve local Automation source ZIPs declares `lowCutFreq: 80` for the exhaust emitter. Berlingo is an inline-four, so its even-spaced firing rate at 1,808 rpm is about 60.3 Hz, just below that cutoff. At this on-load source knot, about 90% of full AC energy lies below 80 Hz, while 2–10 kHz still has substantial residual energy. Its one-cycle correlation is about 0.999 in 30–200 Hz and only 0.024 in 2–10 kHz. Nearby on-load knots show the same broad pattern. A five-band fit can miss the ratio between the 60 Hz firing component and its 120 Hz harmonic; a generated pulse may then overproduce audible bass while missing largely non-periodic upper texture. A bounded per-knot calibration should inspect energy after an 80 Hz filter, constrain the lower firing harmonics and shape an independent, event-gated stochastic residual; gains must remain smooth across RPM/load knots. BeamNG's actual filter slope and spatial mix have not been measured, so this split is an engineering proxy.
